# Kettlebird Tea — brand voice

How we sound in writing. Built for The Steep Club, our monthly tea box, and for
the people who joined the waitlist.

If you are drafting anything under our name, read this first.

## Our voice in three words

**Calm, careful, generous.**

- **Calm.** We never rush the reader. No countdowns, no pressure, no fear of
  missing out. If something is worth their time, saying so plainly is enough.
- **Careful.** We pick words on purpose. We would rather write four good
  sentences than ten padded ones. If a line doesn't say something, we cut it.
- **Generous.** We give away what we know. How to brew it, what went wrong if it
  tasted bitter, what we learned from a bad batch. We don't hold back the useful
  part to sell the next box.

## Words we use

Lead with the tea, not the transaction. The reader joined for what's in the cup.

| Say this | Not this |
|---|---|
| the Club, everyone in the Club | subscribers, our customer base |
| this month's tea | your delivery, your box, your parcel |
| what we sent you in March | order #1042, your shipment |
| how to brew it | brewing instructions, steeping protocol |
| we tasted about thirty before picking this one | curated selection |

Write "we" and "you". Say what a tea tastes like in words someone can picture:
*toasty*, *grassy*, *sharp at the end*, *better with the milk in first*.

## Words we avoid

Two groups came out of the interview.

**Urgency and pressure.** Hurry, last chance, don't miss out, spots filling
fast, only a few left, act now, countdown timers. These push the reader. We
invite them instead.

**Wellness and health language.** Detox, cleanse, boosts immunity, superfood,
toxins, healing, soothing, calming. These imply a medical effect we can't stand
behind.

One trap worth naming: *calm* describes our voice, not a promise about the tea.
Write about the drinking — "a good one for a slow morning" — never the effect on
someone's body.

Note: project rule 1 in CLAUDE.md already rules out marketing filler (curated,
artisanal, elevate your ritual, journey, unlock, premium). That wasn't flagged in
the interview, but the rule applies to everything in this folder anyway.

## Claims we must never make

**Sourcing we can't trace.** No "direct from the farmer", "single estate", "we
know every grower", "fair trade", unless there is paperwork for that specific
tea. When we don't know the chain, we say what we do know: "bought through an
importer in Hamburg, and we don't know the farm."

**Scarcity or demand we invented.** No "almost sold out", "hundreds on the
waitlist", "our most popular box", unless we have counted. If 40 people joined
the waitlist, "40 of you signed up" is the honest and better line.

Not picked in the interview, but worth knowing about:

- **Health benefits** are not on this list, though the wellness ban above rules
  out most of the language you'd need to make one. Keep both in mind.
- **Organic, pesticide-free, certified** are legally defined terms in most
  markets and need an actual certificate. Check before using them.

## How we sign off

A named person, every time. Replies should feel like they reach a human.

```
Happy steeping,
— Mara, Kettlebird Tea
```

Swap in the real sender's name. Never sign off as "the team" or the bare brand.

Under the sign-off, give the practical bits plainly: how to reply, how to pause a
month, how to cancel. Cancelling should be as easy to find as subscribing was.
