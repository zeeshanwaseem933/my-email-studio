# The Steep Club — waitlist launch

Plan written 15 Nov 2026. Email only. Nothing in this plan sends mail; the drafts
come to Mara to send.

## Read this part first

Three things need saying before the plan makes sense.

**The goal is above what email alone usually does.** 300 subscriptions from 2,000
waitlist members means 15% of the whole list joins and pays. For a warm list that
asked to hear from us, a strong email-only launch usually lands somewhere between
5% and 9% — about 100 to 180 people. Getting to 300 needs roughly double the best
normal result. The plan below aims at 300 and the arithmetic for it is in
[Success metrics](#7-success-metrics), along with the four levers that would close
the gap. Worth deciding up front whether 300 is the target we're judged on or the
number we're reaching for.

**The four weeks run straight through Christmas.** Mon 7 Dec to Sun 3 Jan. That
cuts both ways: gift buyers are actually shopping, and everyone's inbox is full.
The calendar sends nothing on 24, 25 or 26 December, and nothing on 1 January.

**Every product fact below is made up.** Asked for working numbers, so the plan now
runs on placeholders — price, teas, tasting notes, cutoff dates, group sizes. They
are in the right ballpark and they make the calendar and the arithmetic work. None
of them is true. See the table in the next section before quoting any of it.

## 1. Campaign overview

**Name:** The Steep Club opens

**In one sentence:** Tell 2,000 people who asked to be told that the Club is open,
show them what's in the cup, and make joining and leaving equally easy to find.

**Primary objective:** 300 waitlist members start a subscription between Mon 7 Dec
2026 and Sun 3 Jan 2027.

**Secondary objectives:**

- Every one of the 2,000 hears from us at least once, in a way that fits which
  group they're in.
- Gift buyers get the shipping cutoff early enough to act on it.
- The list ends the month smaller but warmer: people who don't want this should
  find it easy to leave.
- We learn which of the three groups actually converts, so the next launch isn't
  guesswork.

## 2. Audience

About 2,000 people. They signed up themselves, so nobody here is cold. The counts
below come from the placeholder table and are invented.

**Tea-curious beginners** (780)

New to loose leaf. They want someone to tell them what to buy and how to make it
without feeling talked down to. Their worry is wasting money on something they'll
brew wrong. The generous half of our voice is aimed squarely at them: tell them how
to brew it, tell them what went wrong if it was bitter.

**Gift buyers** (460)

Often not tea drinkers. They need three facts fast: price, what arrives, when it
arrives. They have two real deadlines — 11 Dec for the EU, 18 Dec for the UK — and stating
a deadline is a fact rather than pressure, which keeps it inside the voice guide. One person here can start two or
three subscriptions, which matters for the count.

**Daily tea drinkers** (690)

They already buy tea. They'll compare us on value and on whether the tea is good.
They don't need brewing help and will notice if we talk to them like beginners.
Their objection is commitment — they want to know they can skip a month.

**Where they are:** in their inbox. That's the whole channel plan, so each send has
to earn its place.

**Buying stage:** consideration, all three groups. They've shown interest and
haven't paid. Nobody needs convincing that tea exists.

## 3. Key messages

**Core message:** Good tea, picked carefully, with everything we know about how to
drink it — and you can pause or stop whenever you like.

**Supporting messages:**

1. *We taste a lot before we pick one.* Proof: say how many teas we tried for this
   month's box, if we counted. If we didn't count, say what we did do instead.
2. *We tell you how to brew it, and what to do if it goes wrong.* Proof: real
   brewing notes in the emails themselves, not behind a signup.
3. *Stopping is as easy as starting.* Proof: the pause and cancel steps printed
   under the sign-off of every email.
4. *You know what's coming.* Proof: name the tea in the first box and describe how
   it tastes in plain words.

**By group:**

- Beginners: lead with brewing. The message is "you won't get this wrong."
- Gift buyers: lead with the three facts and the cutoff date. Short emails.
- Daily drinkers: lead with the tea itself and with pausing.

**Lines that must not appear** (from `brand/voice.md`): no hurry, last chance, spots
filling, almost sold out, most popular. No detox, cleanse, boosts immunity,
soothing, calming. No direct from the farmer, single estate, fair trade, organic or
certified unless there is paperwork for that specific tea. No curated, artisanal,
elevate, journey, premium. Say "the Club" and "this month's tea", not "subscribers"
and "your box". Sign off as a named person.

## 4. Channel strategy

Email only, by instruction. One channel, so the work goes into segmentation and
into the copy rather than into spread.

| Channel | Why | Format | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email to the waitlist | It's the only list we have and these people asked for it | Plain, mostly text, one link | Medium — seven drafts |
| Signup page | Not a channel, but the emails are useless without somewhere to land | One page, price and cancel terms visible | Medium, must be live before 7 Dec |
| Reply handling | Our voice invites replies; someone has to answer them | Mara's inbox | Low, ongoing |

Not doing: paid ads, social, PR. No budget and no time inside four weeks.

A note on volume: seven emails across four weeks, but nobody gets all seven. Each
person gets three or four, because the group-specific sends only go to their group,
and anyone who joins drops out of the campaign immediately.

## 7. Success metrics

No analytics connector is authorized in this session — Klaviyo, HubSpot and
Amplitude all need connecting before they can be queried. So there are no
historical benchmarks behind these numbers. The rates below are general email
benchmarks for a warm, opted-in list, not Kettlebird's own figures. Replace them
with our real numbers after Week 1.

**Primary KPI:** 300 subscriptions started by 3 Jan 2027.

**What 300 requires.** Working backwards from 2,000 people:

- A warm list across a seven-email campaign: maybe 10–20% click through at least
  once. That's 200–400 people on the page.
- A dedicated page for people who already asked to be here: maybe 30–40% of them
  join. That's roughly **60 to 160 subscriptions.**
- 300 needs either far more clicks or far better page conversion than that. It's
  about double a good result.

**The four levers that could close the gap:**

1. **Gift buyers starting more than one.** The single biggest one. If 80 of the 460
   gift buyers average 1.8 subscriptions each, that's 144 on its own.
2. **Re-sending to non-openers.** Same email, new subject line, 3 days later. Adds
   roughly 20–30% more opens for one hour of work.
3. **Asking new members to pass it on.** Fits the generous voice. No incentive
   needed, just "if you know someone who'd like this."
4. **Fixing the page, not the emails.** If the page converts at 25% rather than
   40%, no amount of email copy makes up the difference. Watch this number first.

Suggested framing: **160 is the base case, 300 is the stretch.** Set both.

**Secondary KPIs**

| Metric | Target | How we track it |
|---|---|---|
| Subscriptions by group | Known for all three | Signup source tag per email |
| Unique click rate | 15% or better over the campaign | ESP, per send |
| Signup page conversion | 35% or better | Page views vs. signups |
| Unsubscribe rate | Under 1% per send | ESP. Above 2% means we stop and rewrite |
| Replies | Counted and all answered | Mara's inbox |
| Gift vs. own | Known | Checkout field |

**Reporting:** a short look every Monday during the campaign — Dec 14, 21, 28 and
Jan 4. Full write-up in the week of 4 Jan.

## 8. Budget

Small, email only. The costs are time and the sending tool.

- Copy and review: about 3 days of work across the four weeks, most of it before
  7 Dec.
- Signup page: 1–2 days if the page is being built from scratch.
- Sending tool: whatever the plan costs at 2,000 contacts. Roughly 9,000 sends
  across the month, which is inside most entry tiers.
- Reply handling: 30 minutes a day during Weeks 1 and 2.
- No media spend.

Hold back about a day of the copy time. If Week 1 tells us something — one group
converting far better than the others — we want the hours to act on it.

## 9. Risks

**The 300 target.** It's above what this channel usually delivers from this list
size. If it's a hard commitment, the plan is short by roughly half.
*What to do:* agree base and stretch now. If 300 is firm, the honest answer is that
email alone to 2,000 people probably won't get there, and the plan needs either a
bigger list or a second channel.

**Christmas.** Open rates drop in the last two weeks of December, and gift buying
stops dead after the shipping cutoff.
*What to do:* front-load. E1, E2 and E3 all land before 18 Dec, and the gift thread
is finished before the cutoff rather than after it.

**The numbers in this plan are invented.** All of them — price, the three teas and
how they taste, the count of 28, the sourcing lines, both cutoff dates, the group
sizes. They're here so the plan can be built. If one reaches a reader unchanged, we
have made a claim about a product we can't stand behind, which is the thing the
voice guide is strictest about. The cutoff dates are the worst case: a reader can
act on those and have Christmas go wrong.
*What to do:* Mara replaces the table with real figures before any draft goes out,
and each draft gets checked against the table rather than against memory. Brand
review catches voice, not arithmetic — so somebody has to check the numbers on
purpose.

**Pressure creeping in.** A launch with a target and a deadline pulls hard toward
countdowns and "last chance", which the voice guide rules out.
*What to do:* brand review on every draft. The one genuine deadline — the shipping
cutoff — gets stated once per email as a fact, with no adjectives attached.

**The list has gone cold.** Some of these 2,000 signed up a long time ago and may
not remember us.
*What to do:* E1 says who we are in its first line and doesn't assume they
remember. Watch the first unsubscribe rate; it tells us how old this list really is.
